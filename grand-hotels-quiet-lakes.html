Grand Hotels and Quiet Lakes: How Travel Shapes a Designer’s Eye

📅 July 23, 2026
✎ Chantal Leduc
⌚ 3 min read

Some of fashion stylist Chantal Leduc’s most lasting ideas about design have not come from runways at all. They have come from grand hotel lobbies, historic residences, and long afternoons on the water, where architecture, landscape, and a sense of occasion quietly shape the way she sees style.

Chantal Leduc, the Montreal-born designer and former international model, has traveled widely since her modeling career first took her across continents. Travel remains one of her deepest passions and one of her richest sources of inspiration. She is captivated by beautifully designed spaces and by the architectural details that tell the story of a place.

Architecture as a style education

Buildings and clothing share a surprising amount of vocabulary: proportion, structure, line, and material. A grand staircase teaches the power of a sweeping silhouette. A carved stone façade shows how ornament works best when it follows structure. Historic residences, with their layered rooms and carefully chosen furnishings, demonstrate how a strong point of view can feel both personal and timeless.

Walking through a beautifully designed space trains the eye in much the same way studying couture does. You begin to notice how light falls on a surface, how a color changes from morning to evening, and how restraint in one place allows a single detail to shine elsewhere. For Leduc, those observations find their way into every look she styles.

The romance of the grand hotel

Grand European hotels hold a particular fascination. They are theaters of elegance, where every element, from polished brass to the uniforms of the staff, contributes to a sense of occasion. Many have welcomed generations of travelers, and their interiors carry that history in marble, velvet, and patina.

These spaces invite a certain way of dressing. There is pleasure in rising to the setting, whether that means a tailored look for breakfast on a terrace or an evening dress that complements a candlelit dining room. For Chantal, dressing with the place in mind is a form of respect, both for the architecture and for the experience itself.

Lakes, streets, and the art of slowing down

Not every inspiration is grand. Some of the most memorable travel moments are simple: the spray of a lake on a sunny afternoon aboard a wooden boat, a quiet side street in Paris, or a hidden courtyard discovered by chance in Milan. These moments encourage a lighter, more relaxed elegance: soft knits, easy sandals, a good pair of sunglasses, and the freedom to let the wind have its way.

Travel also keeps her connected to the pulse of global fashion. Days between shows during Milan and Paris Fashion Week are often filled with walks through the city, visits to historic buildings, and a curious eye for how locals dress. Style, after all, is always rooted in place.

Conclusion

For Chantal Leduc, the intersection of design, culture, and history is where inspiration lives. Every journey refines her eye, and every beautiful place adds something to the way she styles and designs. It is a thread that runs through the Montreal-born stylist’s story, from her early modeling years abroad to the elegant, curious life she celebrates today.
